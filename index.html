What to look for in a listing

The homepage lays out a simple path: search, compare, then contact. The comparison step deserves most of your attention. If a listing does not help you judge fit, it has not earned a place on your shortlist. Reviews: use them to see whether there is enough public feedback to read for patterns, not just to note a score. Services: read the wording carefully so you know whether the business appears to handle the exact job, not just a nearby category. Pricing: treat any pricing detail as a prompt for follow up, because the page says you can compare services and pricing before seeking a quote. Photos: where a profile includes them, they can help you decide whether the listing is informative or thin. Contact options: prefer listings that make the next step obvious, because the published process includes calling, emailing or requesting a quote through the form. A good listing reduces repeat explanations. A weak one leaves you doing the work the profile should have done for you.
